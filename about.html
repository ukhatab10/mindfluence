About Mindfluence

Mindfluence exists for one reason: the psychology behind buying is usually explained in academic language or in hype. We explain it in plain English — what each idea is, the study or book it comes from, and where you meet it in everyday life.

What We Cover

Our guides cover the ideas that decide how people choose, pay, and stay loyal:

  • Pricing psychology — anchoring, charm pricing, and the decoy effect: how the way a price is shown changes what people are willing to pay.
  • Influence — social proof and scarcity: why other people’s choices and the fear of missing out move us.
  • Decision-making — loss aversion and the sunk cost fallacy: why losses hurt more than gains feel good, and why money already spent still steers us.
  • Ownership and value — the IKEA effect: why we value things more when we had a hand in making them.
  • Strategy — Purple Cow and Blue Ocean Strategy: why being remarkable beats being slightly better, and how new markets are created instead of fought over.

How We Check Our Facts

Every idea on this site traces back to published research or a named book: Tversky & Kahneman on anchoring, Cialdini on influence, Ariely on the decoy and IKEA effects, Thaler on sunk costs, Godin on remarkable marketing, and Kim & Mauborgne on blue ocean strategy. Every article lists its sources at the bottom.

If a finding cannot be traced to its original study or book, we leave it out rather than guess. If a famous result has been questioned or is hard to replicate, we say so. Popular summaries of psychology are often exaggerated — we try not to be one of them.

What We Are Not

We are not affiliated with any of the researchers, authors, or publishers named on this site. We do not sell courses, consulting, coaching, or marketing services. No company pays us to recommend anything, and no article on this site is sponsored.

We are also not your business advisor. Our articles are general education, written to help you understand how buying decisions work — so you can spot these ideas as a shopper, or use them honestly as a seller.

Corrections

Research moves on, and mistakes happen. If you spot an error, a misattributed study, or an out-of-date claim, please tell us through the contact page. We check every report against the original source and correct confirmed errors promptly.
